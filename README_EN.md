1. Why This Tool Was Created

In everyday life, science, and interactions with artificial intelligence, we are accustomed to evaluating statements through a subjective binary of "true or false." However, fluent text often masks the absence of real action:
• wishes and declarations are presented as ready plans;
• partial coincidences or numerical checks are passed off as proven universal laws;
• passive constructions like "it happened by itself," "time flows," or "life emerged" conceal the absence of an agent and a physical mechanism.
Case Filter is a strict accounting protocol based on the Case Logic Method and formal act/predictor formulas. The instrument does not "guess the truth" or solve problems on behalf of a human—it operates as an impartial registrar that audits every record for backing by real action, resources, and time.
The core property of the instrument is autonomy. Because it is grounded in a formal formula, it can be used on paper (in a notebook or ledger) or loaded into a session with any AI model. Artificial intelligence is not treated as a "source of truth," but merely as one of several interchangeable carriers of the protocol.

2. Mathematical Framework of the Method

Every statement is treated in this method as a transaction record. To become a legal fact, a record must pass through a formal verification loop.

The Act Formula

Ω(T) --[act σ(O)]--> E ∈ σ(O)

The Predictor Formula

Ω(T) --[act σ(O)]--> Ê ∈ σ(O) --[verification]--> E
Element Definitions:
• Ω(T) — Frame: what is posited in the task, the domain with parameter T;
• [act σ(O)] — Act at Position: who performs the action and where, paid for with real ticks (resources, labor, computation, experiment);
• Ê — Candidate (Forecast): issued strictly before the act and strictly with a dated verification window;
• E — Act Result: the actual outcome matched against the candidate by date;
• ∈ σ(O) — Position Membership: without specifying the position and record holder, there is neither an event nor accounting.
Verification Rules:
• If Ê = E — the record returns to the Dative case as a fact (representing the elimination of false alternatives, not an addition of knowledge).
• If Ê ≠ E — the false candidate is cleared; the [P] (working version) status remains.
• A contribution without a future act produces an unverifiable forecast ([P] until the end of time — left boundary).
• A record with neither contribution nor act is routed to Cell 1 (right boundary).

3. The Case X-Ray: How a Record Is Audited

Every incoming entry is audited through a system of seven grammatical case questions:
1. Prepositional Case ("Where is the transaction?" / position σ(O)): Who holds the record, and in which coordinate system is it made? Without a position, there is neither an event nor a protocol.
2. Nominative Case ("Who is the executor?"): Who is the debtor paying with ticks for the result? The executor does not predict—the executor acts.
3. Instrumental Case ("Paid with what? By means of what?"): What currency, work, or physical mechanism backs the event? If a text uses a passive construction ("occurred," "shines," "emerged"), the instrument demands the hidden Instrumental case.
4. Genitive Case ("Where does the contribution come from?"): Which prior record serves as the source of the forecast or the basis for the future act?
5. Accusative Case-Ê ("What is being created?"): The future target, a candidate prior to the act (kept on a separate line from the result).
6. Accusative Case-E ("What has been created?"): The actual outcome of a completed act.
7. Dative Case ("To whom is the record credited?"): The creditor; fixation of ownership after the transaction and closing of the verification window.

4. Laws of the Chair and the Error "Zoo"

To prevent the carrier (human or neural network) from substituting facts with speculation, the instrument enforces 7 Laws of the Chair:
1. The chair holds the frame: "Who" in a record is the position upon which action is placed, not the substrate.
2. Passive voice carries a hidden Instrumental: Every impersonal formulation must answer "by means of what?"
3. Slot and chair are distinct lines: An empty slot for a solution cannot be presented as the solution itself.
4. Impersonality: The subject of a decision is never entered into the protocol card.
5. Incompleteness ≠ Falsehood: An open slot is simply an open slot, not a verdict of "false."
6. Ban on window merging: Two records with different dated windows cannot be merged into one cell; contradiction arises only from improper merging.
7. Analogy is an operation on records, not a cell: Every card is audited strictly on its own text, not by template from another card.
If a record violates frame rules, it is routed to the Anomaly Registry ("The Zoo"):
• Cell 1: Record without an act (text exists, but neither movement nor a verification window is present).
• Cell 13: Impostor time (reference to time without specifying a process and payment in ticks).
• Cell 14: Impostor event (passive "happened" or "emerged" without presenting the hidden Instrumental case).
• Cell 15: Candidate carrier beyond the edge of the record (e.g., "the sun will rise tomorrow" holds [P] status until tomorrow arrives).

5. Role Discipline When Working with AI

The core flaw of modern large language models is hallucination and self-evaluation: the AI sets its own task, solves it, and declares its own output "true."
Case Filter blocks this through strict role separation:
• Key Holder (Human): Only the human is authorized to issue FORECAST and VERIFICATION commands and assign dated windows.
• Carrier (AI): Must transcribe the input into a protocol card, log its own status (carrier: AI as an independence constraint), and is strictly prohibited from initiating bets, proposing windows, or using words like "proven," "confirmed," or "truth."
• Legal Refusal: When data is insufficient for classification, the valid output of the instrument is unidentified remainder or [P] candidate. This is a normal operating mode of the filter, not a malfunction.

7. How to Use the Tool

1. Open the Russian protocol file Инструмент_Падежный_фильтр_v2.2.md in this repository (or use the formal protocol commands).
2. Paste the protocol at the very beginning of a new session with any AI model (or print the card template for paper use).
3. Submit any statement for audit using: ПРОГОН: [statement, hypothesis, or problem]
4. To register a forecast prior to an event (as Key Holder), use: ПРОГНОЗ: [expected outcome] окно [exact date]
5. Once the date arrives, run verification using: СВЕРКА: [forecast vs. actual outcome]
6. To audit carrier discipline over the session, enter: СВОДКА.
